# Third-party data, code and citations

## MIT KEMAR

Gardner, W. G. and Martin, K. D., “HRTF measurements of a KEMAR,” Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 97, 3907–3908 (1995); measurements recorded 1994. Source and terms: https://sound.media.mit.edu/resources/KEMAR.html . Copyright 1994 MIT Media Laboratory; data provided free with no restrictions on use provided the authors are cited.

Our derived JSON retains compact horizontal responses (0° to 180° at 5° steps), original 44.1 kHz/128 samples, signed-16-bit scale divided by 32768. Negative angles swap ears. No individualized measurement or BTE microphone data is implied.

## LibriSpeech

Panayotov, V., Chen, G., Povey, D. and Khudanpur, S., “LibriSpeech: an ASR corpus based on public domain audio books,” ICASSP 2015. https://www.openslr.org/12 . License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ .

Original test-clean utterances 6930-75918-0000 and 6930-75918-0007 are included as FLAC and converted WAV. Both are the same speaker. The browser rescales and loops them. Derived MSBG/NAL-R WAV files are marked by their filenames and generated by research/reference_study.py; they are not original corpus recordings. No endorsement by the corpus authors is implied.

## Clarity

https://github.com/claritychallenge/clarity ; installed evaluator version pyclarity 0.9.0. Its upstream licensing and notices apply to that dependency; no source files are vendored here. NAL-R, HASPI v2 and MSBG are called from the dependency. Citation information is in the white paper. Our browser filter bank is an independent approximation, not a port of the complete MSBG model.

## Literature

Bibliographic references and original URLs are embedded in dist/white-paper.tex and dist/survey-data.json. Published model performance is not presented as a local measurement. Neural weights and paper PDFs are not redistributed.

## RNNoise WASM (edition 0.3)

`dist/vendor/rnnoise-sync.js` is copied unmodified from the official npm package
`@jitsi/rnnoise-wasm@0.2.1`; it includes the compiled default pretrained weights.
Wrapper: Apache-2.0 (`dist/vendor/JITSI-LICENSE`). RNNoise: BSD-3-Clause
(`dist/vendor/RNNOISE-COPYING`), retrieved from the exact pinned submodule.
`dist/vendor/provenance.json` records npm integrity, wrapper/submodule commits,
artifact size and SHA-256. The 2018 paper is background, not a claim that these
are its original weights. No vendor source was modified.

## Version 0.5 additions

No new third-party code, model weights or datasets are bundled. The feedback path, cancellers, HRTF personalization, SOFA converter and tracker are original implementations of published methods:

- van Waterschoot & Moonen (2011), Proc. IEEE 99(2):288–327, doi:10.1109/JPROC.2010.2090998: feedback-control review, MSG/ASG and bias.
- Spriet, Proudler, Moonen & Wouters (2005), IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 53(10):3749–3763: PEM-based adaptive feedback cancellation.
- Yilmaz & Rickard (2004), IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 52(7):1830–1847: time-frequency sparsity of speech, the basis of per-bin voting.
- Kuhn (1977), J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 62(1):157–167, and Algazi, Avendano & Duda (2001), J. Audio Eng. Soc. 49(6):472–479: spherical-head ITD and anthropometry.
- Middlebrooks (1999), J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 106(3):1480–1492: frequency scaling of external-ear transfer functions.
- Burkhard & Sachs (1975), J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 58(1):214–222: the KEMAR manikin. The 15.2 cm head breadth used as the scaling reference is an assumed value.
- AES69 (SOFA) defines the input format read by `research/sofa_to_lab.py`. `h5py` is an optional user-installed dependency, not vendored. Converted HRTF sets carry their source dataset's license; none is committed here.

The synthetic tone complex in `research/feedback-evaluation.mjs` and the synthetic Woodworth-sphere set in `tests/test_sofa_convert.py` are generated by code, not recorded.
